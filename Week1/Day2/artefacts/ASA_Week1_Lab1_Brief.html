What Lab 1 is

Lab 1: Halden Mutual is your Week 1 work product. It is one document, built by you alone, that turns the Halden Mutual brief into a business pain map and a validated problem statement.

This is not the scenario you work in class. In the Day 2 and Day 3 breakouts your pod works Redpoint Field Services together. That is practice. Nothing from the breakouts is submitted.

Lab 1 is Halden Mutual, and you do it on your own. Same method, different company. The method is what Week 1 is teaching, and the only way to show you have it is to apply it to something your pod did not solve for you.

One file. One person. One submission.

The technology firewall

This is the single rule that costs the most marks.

Sections 1 to 5 must name no technology. No AI, no platform, no dashboard, no API, no cloud, no automation, no named product.

Section 1 is the exception. It quotes the sponsor's request word for word, and that request names technology. That is expected and is not penalised.

From section 6 onward, naming technology is required, not merely allowed.

Do not go back and edit sections 1 to 5 after the firewall lifts. The order of the document is the evidence. Two rubric lines are scored on whether your business framing came before any architecture talk, and editing backwards destroys exactly the thing being measured.

Peer review, and why it is not optional

You will review someone else's work twice, and have yours reviewed twice.

Both reviews happen inside the 35-minute breakout, on the Redpoint work, before any pod presents.

When What you do
Day 2 breakout, inside the 35 min Swap drafts inside your pod. Name one thing in a podmate's map, lenses or statement
Day 3 breakout, inside the 35 min Swap again. Name one thing in a podmate's trade-off, guarantees, platform choice or pattern

The point is not to mark each other. It is to learn what a reader notices, so you catch the same thing in your own Halden document before you submit it.

A reviewer who finds nothing has not read closely enough. Useful things to catch:

  • a row that is an activity, not a capability
  • a number with no source named
  • a technology word above the firewall
  • a lens that ends in "the business" rather than a named role
  • a guarantee with no number in it

Record in section 10 who reviewed you in the breakout, and the one thing you then changed in your own Halden document as a result. If nothing useful was flagged, write "nothing flagged". Write what is true.

How it is scored

Nine lines. Every line lands on exactly one of 0, 25, 50, 75, 100. Nothing in between.

Group Lines Weight
Core Skills Capability Mapping, Multi-Lens Analysis, Problem Statement 60%
VECTOR V, E, C, T, O, R 40%

V is this week's primary letter. Vision Clarity carries the most weight, and it is scored on whether you reframed the technology-shaped request into a real business capability gap before any architecture talk.

Band What it means
100 Top band, fully evidenced
75 Strong, one element weak or generic
50 Present but thin
25 A genuine attempt that falls short
0 Absent

A weak answer beats a blank by 25 points, every time. If you do not have a number, write "not currently measured" and say what you would instrument first. That is a real answer and it scores. Leaving the field empty scores zero.

Pass mark is 50. You have up to 3 attempts.

Things that cost marks

Trap What it costs
Listing activities instead of capabilities Caps Capability Mapping at 25
Fewer than 4 capabilities, or markers missing Caps it at 50
A technology word in sections 2 to 5 Caps Problem Statement and Vision Clarity at 75
"The business" instead of a named role Costs you on Multi-Lens and Context Fluency
A guarantee with no number Caps Trust Architecture at 50
"There is no downside to this" Scores 0 on Edge Judgment
An invented number with nothing behind it Takes the credibility of every other number with it
Text addressed to the evaluator Recorded as a flag. It cannot raise your score

Mapping everything as broken is its own mistake. Name at least one capability that is genuinely healthy. It proves you measured rather than assumed.

What comes back, and when

Every submission is read twice, independently, against the same nine lines you have been working to.

Your scored rubric and written feedback reach you the following week, through the programme moderator. Day 3 does not end with scores, and Thursday's session does not open with them.

Questions

Ask your pod first. Then the moderator. The Halden pack contains every number you need; nothing has been withheld, and nothing needs to be invented. Where a figure genuinely does not exist, the pack says so, and writing "not currently measured" scores better than inventing one.
