Before you read this

This is not the Redpoint scenario. Redpoint was the exercise you worked through together in class. Halden is yours alone.

You work the same method on different material. That is the point: the method is what Week 1 is teaching, and the only way to show you have it is to apply it to something your pod did not solve for you.

Everything you need is in this pack. No figure has been withheld, and nothing needs to be invented. Where a number does not exist, that absence is stated plainly, and saying so in your submission scores better than making one up.

The company

Halden Mutual underwrites commercial property insurance for mid-sized businesses: warehouses, light industrial units, retail parks and small manufacturing sites. It is a mutual, so its policyholders are its members, and it has no external shareholders pressing for growth.
Policies in force 61,000
Claims notified per year 14,800
Claims staff 240, of whom 84 are licensed adjusters
Offices Three, plus a remote adjuster network
Regulated by A national financial conduct regulator

In 2023 Halden acquired a competitor's book of 19,000 policies. Those policies, and their documents, still sit in the acquired company's policy system. The two systems will run in parallel until at least 2027. Nobody disputes that this is a problem; the integration budget has been deferred twice.

The request on your desk

An email, forwarded to you this morning by the Chief Operating Officer, with no additional comment:

"I have been reading about what the insurtechs are doing with AI claims handling. Several of them now auto-settle low-value claims in under an hour with no human involvement at all. Our members wait weeks. I want us to build an AI claims engine that auto-settles anything under 25,000, and I want a plan for it before the next board meeting. This feels like exactly the kind of thing we should have done two years ago."

That is a prescription. Your job this week is to produce the diagnosis.

Constraints

These are facts about Halden, not opinions. Any answer that ignores them is answering a different question.

Constraint Detail
Regulatory explainability The regulator requires that any automated decision affecting a member outcome can be explained to that member and appealed. This is not negotiable and is actively supervised
Two policy systems The acquired book stays in a separate system until at least 2027. Any solution must work across both or explicitly scope itself to one
Scarce licensed adjusters 84 licensed adjusters is the binding capacity constraint. Hiring takes 9 to 14 months including licensing
Mutual, not a growth business There is no shareholder mandate for speed. Member outcomes and reserve stability are what the board is measured on
Deferred integration budget The system integration has been deferred twice. Proposing it as a precondition without saying what happens if it is deferred again is not a plan

Six questions worth answering before you write

These are not submission fields. They are the questions a good consultant would ask before touching the template.

  1. The COO wants to auto-settle claims under 25,000. Those are 71% of volume but 19% of value. What does that tell you about where the money actually is?
  2. Settlement takes 47 days against a benchmark of 29. Which capability is consuming the difference? It is not the settling.
  3. If you auto-settled a claim before cover was validated, what exactly would you be paying for?
  4. Which capability is genuinely healthy? There is at least one. Naming it is what proves you measured rather than assumed.
  5. 61% of fraud flags are overturned, and each flag adds 9 days. What is the current fraud rule actually costing, and who pays it?
  6. Recoveries leave an estimated 4.1 million a year on the table and the team says it is capacity. Is that a capability problem or a priority problem, and how would you tell the difference?

Two traps

The first trap is agreeing with the COO. Auto-settlement is a real technique and it works in some businesses. The question is whether it addresses where Halden's cost and delay actually concentrate. The data answers that. Follow the data, not the seniority.

The second trap is a map where everything is broken. One capability here is performing well. A map that marks all six as problems tells your reader you did not look. Say which is fine, and say why you are confident.

What counts as a good number

From Day 2, unchanged:

Kind Example How it scores
Sourced "31% of acquired-book claims, from the claims system" Best. A reviewer can check it
Flagged "Roughly a third, assumption to confirm against the document log" Honest, and it scores
Invented A confident figure with nothing behind it Fatal. It takes every other number with it

One figure in this pack is explicitly not currently measured. Writing "not currently measured, and I would instrument it first" is a strong answer, not a weak one.
